    • Musk's Possession of Twitter

    Elon Musk’s possession of Twitter highlights significant concerns about the platform’s content moderation policies. Musk laid off multiple longtime key executives including CEO Parag Agrawal, CFO Ned Segal, and then the head of legal policy, trust, and safety, Vijaya Gadde who played a pivotal role in banning former President Donald Trump from the platform. His dismantling of a team who focused on such moderation is no longer at Twitter and his more lenient approach to certain types of speech has led to an increased use of hate speech and misinformation on the platform. His permissive stance on content moderation has allowed alt-right groups to express their views more freely without the fear of censorship, which has created a more welcoming environment for such content. Musk’s Twitter emphasizes engagement between users over content quality, meaning that algorithms that prioritize likes, retweets, and comments, which tends to often include misinformation circulate freely around the platform. Musk then offers an $8 a month verification process for the elusive blue Twitter check mark, instead of the traditional merit-based system, which is given to a user based on their follower account and prominence in fields like journalism, academia, or entertainment. This approach quickly proved problematic, as it led to the creation of fake accounts, and the greater visibility of high-profile accounts that share extremist views which then attracted more followers and legitimizes their perspectives. Without sufficient monitoring and moderation, the online environment can become hostile, hindering the ability of marginalized groups to organize and advocate for their rights.
